How we work 
We sit with the team, learn how the business runs, then build. 
Six rules. They are the reason a two-person firm can outrun a bigger one on an owner-led engagement. 
 01 
Two founders on every engagement 
The people who scope the work are the people who ship it. Nothing is handed down after the first meeting. 
 02 
Direct access 
You email us and you get us. One address, no ticket queue, no intake form standing between a question and an answer. 
 03 
No account managers 
Nobody bills you to relay a message. The status update comes from the person who did the work that week. 
 04 
No juniors on your budget 
Your engagement is not somebody's training ground. If it gets built, one of the two of us built it. 
 05 
No deck-only strategy 
A slide is not a system. Every engagement ends in software that runs, and you own the code. 
 06 
We learn the business first 
We sit with the team that does the work, watch the process at the speed it really happens, and map where time and money leak. The build starts after that, not before. 
Before you sign 
The five questions owners ask us first. 
Open any of them. The answers are the same ones we give on the call. 
 FIG. 02 
 Working questions 
 What happens in the free audit? 
 + 
We map where time and money leak, then show the arithmetic. You keep the map whether or not you hire us. Nothing is built at this stage and nothing is charged for it. 
 Who owns the code? 
 + 
You do. It is written into the agreement before anything is built. 
 What are you measured against, and when does the clock start? 
 + 
We agree the baseline in writing before anything is built, and you sign off on the exact numbers we are measured against. The clock starts at deployment, not at signature. If the system misses the agreed bar, we keep working at no additional cost until it clears. You can cancel at any time. 
The bar and the timeframe are agreed per engagement. Nothing here guarantees a particular dollar amount. The full version is on how we charge . 
 Can you work inside our data rules? 
 + 
Constraints come first, because they decide the architecture. Uniserve required every model and every byte hosted in Canada, so that requirement shaped the build before a line of it was written. Bring us your hosting and data-residency rules at the start and we design to them. 
 Are we too small for this? 
 + 
Owner-led is the whole point. Our clients run from a multi-venue restaurant group to a Canadian internet and telecom provider. What they share is an owner who can make a decision in one meeting. 
The gap 
The technology was the easy part. The gap is people. 
Models are cheap to buy and hard to adopt. The money goes to the software; the staff who have to use it on a live shift get whatever is left. 
 93 % 
of AI budgets go to technology. 
 Deloitte, 2025 
 7 % 
goes to the people expected to use it. 
 Deloitte, 2025 
That second number is where engagements die. So the first week of ours is spent on the floor, not in a repository: we learn the job as the team does it, in their words, on their screens. Then we build the thing that removes the part they hate, and we stay until they use it without us. 
Where we work 
Owner-led clients across three sectors. 
Hospitality, telecom and municipal politics. Every status below is stated plainly, live, shipped or still in build. 
Hospitality, nightlife and events 
 Live 
Fennec is a full operating system for premium nightlife and event venues: floor plans, reservations, ticketing, bottle service, inventory and live ops, synced with Square. It runs at Harbour Event Centre, TradeX, The Pit UBC and The Show Ottawa, plus more than 20 event companies. Palapa Tours in Ottawa runs floating tiki-bar cruises, where gratuity splits shifted cruise to cruise and caused disputes; we built and shipped tip-out software that calculates, splits and tracks the payouts. B-Side Group is a hospitality group, and we are building them customised inventory management software across three of their restaurant venues: a stock count per site, one catalogue across the venues, the variance between what was bought and what was sold, and reorder points. That one is in build, not delivered, and we are claiming no result from it yet. 
Fennec, live. Palapa Tours, shipped. B-Side Group, in build. 
Telecom and internet 
 In build 
Uniserve Communications Corporation is a Canadian internet and telecom provider. A high volume of repeat customer questions was arriving through their support channels, so we are building them a customer-facing AI chatbot and a voice agent for residential customer support. It is a three-month AI enablement engagement, now in build. Every model and every byte stays hosted in Canada. 
Uniserve Communications Corporation, in build. 
Political and campaign organisations 
 Live 
ABC is a municipal political party in Vancouver. We parse and reconcile the records they already hold, matching them across sources and producing confidence scores so it is clear which records stand up, and we assist with their targeted marketing campaigns off the back of that reconciled set. That work is live. We take no position on the party or its politics, and none is implied by the engagement: the work is data parsing, reconciliation and campaign marketing support. 
ABC, Vancouver, live.
