Before the call 
What to bring 
Three things. Rough is fine. We tighten the numbers together while you are on the call. 
 01 / The workflow that hurts most 
 One process, not a list. The one your team complains about, or the one that only works because a particular person is in the building. Quoting, ticket triage, scheduling, reconciliation, payouts, onboarding, reporting: bring whichever you would fix first. 
 02 / Roughly what it costs today 
 Two numbers get us there: hours a week spent on it, and a loaded hourly rate for the people spending them. Round figures are enough. On the Uniserve programme the same sum reads 1 hour a day x about 250 working days x about $40 an hour, so about $10,000 per technician per year. Projection That figure is a projection for a programme still in build, not a booked result. 
 03 / Whoever else has to say yes 
 The person who actually does the work, and the person who signs. Having both on the first call turns three meetings into one, and it stops us designing something the floor will refuse to use. 
 Not needed 
 No RFP. No requirements document. No stack preference. No budget figure. We do not bring a slide deck either.

Four stages, in this order

 1 
Operations audit 
 The 30 minute call, free 
We map where time and money leak across the workflow you brought, and show the arithmetic first. On the Uniserve programme the same exercise produced a mapped view of where support time was going, and a shortlist of the places AI would actually pay. 
 2 
Scope 
 After the audit 
We write down the one or two builds worth doing, with the numbers beside them. On the Uniserve engagement that came down to a customer-facing chatbot and a voice agent for residential customer support, chosen on return and feasibility. Constraints go in here too: for Uniserve, all data and models had to be hosted in Canada.

 Contact FAQ 
 Is the audit call really free? 
 + 
Yes. Thirty minutes, no cost, no card, no obligation. We map where time and money leak and show the arithmetic first. You keep the map whether or not you hire us. 
 Who will I be talking to? 
 + 
One of the two founders. Shiv Lohia holds a dual BA in Psychology and Business plus a Master of Management from UBC, co-founded Fennec, and designed user experiences at Apply Digital including the Harvard Business School site. Vishal Desh holds Computer Science plus a Master of Management from UBC, works in Python, AWS and Azure, and architected the core of Fennec and its Ferry AI agent network. 
 Who owns the code you write? 
 + 
You do. It goes into the agreement before anything is built, alongside the baseline you sign off on and the right to cancel at any time. 
 What if the system misses the agreed bar? 
 + 
We keep working at no additional cost until it clears. The bar itself is agreed in writing before the build starts and the clock starts at deployment, not at signature. To be plain about the limits: the bar and the timeframe are set per engagement, and nothing here guarantees a particular dollar amount. How we charge, in full. 
 Do you only work with nightlife venues? 
 + 
No. Fennec, the operating system for premium nightlife and event venues, is live across Harbour Event Centre, TradeX, The Pit UBC, The Show Ottawa and 20 or more event companies. Alongside it we are in build with Uniserve Communications, a Canadian ISP and telecom, and with B-Side Group, a hospitality group whose inventory software we are writing. ABC, a municipal political party in Vancouver, is live: we parse and reconcile the records they hold and support their targeted marketing campaigns. We shipped custom tip-out software for Palapa Tours Ottawa, a floating tiki-bar cruise operator. The work, client by client. 
 Can our data stay in Canada? 
 + 
It is a constraint we already build to. On the Uniserve programme, in build now, all data and models must be hosted in Canada, and we built to that requirement from the first day. Bring your constraint to the audit call and it shapes the scope rather than surfacing late. 
 What if I am not sure AI is the answer? 
 + 
Good. About 95% of enterprise AI pilots show no measurable P&L return (MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide", 2025), and companies spend about 93% of AI budgets on technology against about 7% on the people expected to use it. That is why the call starts with your workflow and your arithmetic instead of a tool. Sometimes the answer is a small piece of custom software, and sometimes it is to leave the process alone.
